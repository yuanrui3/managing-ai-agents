# Related work

Literature rechecked 18 November 2026. The verified references overlap individual comparisons. The proposed experiment combines management styles, tier and effort allocation, and four forms of scarcity against an unlimited baseline on the same checked explainer packs. The NeurIPS citation remains unresolved. The expanded [mathematical analysis](mathematical-analysis.md) covers recent resource allocation, evaluation, structured generation, and interruption research with derivations tied to the local pilot.

- Kim et al.'s 8 April 2026 revision compares 260 configurations across six benchmarks. Its cross-validated regression fit is .373, and capability saturation is more robust than several tool-count interactions under benchmark clustering. This motivates testing coordination on packs with both separable work and cross-part dependencies. [Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3)
- Pappu et al.'s 28 May 2026 revision measures incomplete exploitation of expertise. Its reported 6.3–41.1% ML synergy gaps use a per-problem oracle. Revealed-expert teams exceed the best fixed individual on four of five ML benchmarks. The specialist arm here assigns distinct deliverables using independently measured skill. Keep fixed solo selection, feasible skill routing, and hindsight oracle headroom distinct in the analysis. [Multi-Agent Teams Hold Experts Back](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01011v4)
- The brief describes a planner and executor sharing one token budget. The conference URL was inaccessible, and exact-title and conference-ID searches yielded no primary copy. Its methods and overlap remain unverified. [My Budget Is Yours](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/170708)
- BAGEN uses rollout replay to test whether an agent can estimate future spending and detect infeasibility mid-task. It reports systematic optimism. The proposed disclosure intervention supplies the manager with measured remaining resources and tests whether choices improve. [BAGEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00198v1)
- Sun et al. already evaluate starting at low effort and escalating on verified failure. Their policy matched or improved fixed top effort success across the tested models and benchmarks. This study tests that policy alongside tier and vendor selection under distinct scarcity conditions. [What Effort Level Should I Choose?](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34491v1)
- DecisionBench already measures same-vendor delegation against the share expected from roster composition and compares ways of exposing peer profiles. The planned comparison uses skill results and available settings to construct its hiring baseline and varies resource disclosure. [DecisionBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19099)

Two adjacent preprints also narrow the claim. Zhang et al. vary shared token ceilings and planner access to task information on repair tasks. Wang et al. allocate models and parallel samples dynamically under joint budget and deadline constraints on code and proof workflows. [The Organization of Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20449), [On Time, Within Budget](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06110)
